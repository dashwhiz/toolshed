Timestamp converter

A number from a log, or a date from anywhere, read back in whichever timezone you need. It works out whether a number counts seconds, milliseconds or nanoseconds rather than assuming and landing you in the wrong millennium.

Nothing is uploaded. The conversion happens in this page.

    What this cannot tell you.
    • Which unit a bare number was meant to be. The reading is inferred from its size, and the other plausible reading is shown beside it so you can tell them apart yourself.
    • Whether a date written without a timezone meant your zone or somewhere else. JavaScript reads a date on its own as UTC and a date with a time as local, which is a real difference of hours — the reading used is always stated.
    • Epoch time has no leap seconds. Every day in it is exactly 86,400 seconds, so a converted instant can sit a second away from what an atomic clock recorded.
    • Zone offsets come from this device's own database. Historic dates and future political changes are where those disagree between systems.
